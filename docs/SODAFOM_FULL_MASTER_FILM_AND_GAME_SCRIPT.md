# SODAFOM — FULL MASTER FILM + GAME WALKTHROUGH SCRIPT

Target: one connected 3D world, approximately 11 minutes total, with natural walking transitions so it feels like one village/town rather than separate screens.

## Character lock
- Michael Davis: bald/closely shaved head, neat dark beard, smart navy suit, warm friendly look. Archie’s dad and sole Founder of Sodafom.
- Archie: blond hair, bright emerald-green eyes, same blue-and-gold Sodafom outfit. Caring, curious eight-year-old boy voice.
- Rob: brown wide-brim hat, glasses, grey moustache/short grey beard, cream shirt, brown waistcoat. Gentle older British storyteller voice.
- Jessica: black Cockapoo with small white chin. Sally: black Cockapoo. Daisy: brown/white Cockapoo.
- Dogs bark only: friendly, soft, playful.

## Production rule
Every scene must connect physically to the next. Characters walk through gates, paths, doors, bridges, corridors and streets instead of cutting to unrelated backgrounds. Re-use matching paving, lamps, blue/gold signs, flowers, harbour details and village architecture so the whole film feels like one connected Sodafom place.

---

## SCENE 1 — FOUNDER INTRO + ORFORD VILLAGE + SWEET SHOP (about 1 minute)
Camera tracks Michael and Archie walking down a warm cobblestone street. Dogs trot beside them. Shopkeepers wave, flags move, flowers sway and warm window lights flicker.

MICHAEL: “Hello, I’m Michael Davis, the founder of Sodafom, and I’d like to introduce you to Sodafom and Archie.”

ARCHIE: “Hi! Come on — let’s explore, learn, play and have an adventure together!”

Archie points to the sweet shop. They walk inside naturally. Jars line the walls. The shop feels busy and alive.

MICHAEL: “Archie, if we have twelve sweets and share them between four friends, how many does each friend get?”

Twelve sweets visually group into four sets of three.

ARCHIE: “Three each!”

MICHAEL: “Exactly. That’s how Sodafom turns everyday adventures into learning.”

Rob appears at the doorway, smiling and nodding.

ROB: “Maths can be hiding anywhere — even in a sweet jar.”

Archie walks past books, bags and school items and points them out.

ARCHIE: “There are books, games, school things and lots more to discover.”

SFX: village ambience, shop bell, jars softly clinking, quiet happy dog bark.

GAME MIRROR: player walks Archie into the shop; tap jars; complete sharing/fractions/money challenges to unlock the exit gate and collect a star.

---

## SCENE 2 — ORFORD QUAY / MAIN WORLD HUB (about 1 minute)
Archie leads Michael and Rob out of the shop and down the same street toward the harbour. The road opens naturally onto the quay. Boats rock gently, gulls pass overhead and water reflects the blue/gold signs.

ARCHIE: “This is where our adventures join together. We can go to lessons, games, reading, homework help, the museum, theatre, sports and more.”

Rob points to signposts built into the world, not floating menus.

ROB: “Choose a path and the learning carries on with you.”

Michael points to an Ask Archie kiosk.

MICHAEL: “Children can ask questions by voice or typing, and Archie helps step by step.”

Archie demonstrates a microphone button.

ARCHIE: “You can talk to me too. I’ll help you think it through.”

GAME MIRROR: the quay is the walkable central hub. Roads visibly lead to the school, library, sports centre, museum, theatre, fairground and nature area. Progress carries between areas.

SFX: water, gulls, footsteps, distant boat bell.

---

## SCENE 3 — SOLAR SYSTEM NIGHT-SKY WALK (about 1 minute)
They walk through a lit arch from the quay onto a dark stargazing terrace. The sky opens above them. Telescope nearby. Dogs sit and look up.

ARCHIE: “Rob, do all the planets move?”

ROB: “They do. Every planet travels around the Sun, and the Moon travels around the Earth.”

All eight planets appear clearly. Each rotates on its own axis and moves around the Sun. The Moon visibly orbits Earth. No distracting hard orbit lines.

ARCHIE: “Wow! Space is always moving!”

Rob points out Earth, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn. A shooting star crosses the sky.

ROB: “And every question can take us somewhere new.”

GAME MIRROR: player walks between planet stations; correct science questions make a planet rotate, reveal a fact or unlock the next telescope marker.

SFX: soft cosmic ambience, shooting-star shimmer, telescope click.

---

## SCENE 4 — SPORTS CENTRE + FOOTBALL + BASKETBALL + SWIMMING (about 1 minute)
A path from the stargazing terrace slopes toward a bright sports complex. Outdoor pitches are visible first. Archie runs ahead, then slows so Michael and Rob catch up.

ARCHIE: “Here we learn while we move!”

Football challenge: a question appears on a pitch-side board.

ROB: “Eight players split into two equal teams. How many on each team?”

ARCHIE: “Four!”

He kicks the ball into the goal. Crowd cheer.

They walk indoors to basketball. Archie answers a quick number question and shoots a basket.

Then they pass the swimming pool. Water moves naturally.

ARCHIE: “Games can use maths, reading, science and teamwork — and every level gets harder as you improve.”

GAME MIRROR: correct answer triggers a pass, shot, swim boost or celebration. Microphone answers supported. Wrong answer pauses action and Archie gives a hint.

SFX: whistle, ball kick, net swish, pool water, quiet cheering.

---

## SCENE 5 — MAGICAL LIBRARY + READING (about 1 minute)
The sports-centre corridor opens onto a quieter garden path leading to a grand library door. Archie pushes it open. Books float gently but the room stays cosy rather than chaotic.

ARCHIE: “This is my favourite place.”

Rob takes a glowing book from a shelf.

ROB: “Stories can come alive — but you still do the reading.”

A sentence highlights word by word. Correct spoken words turn green; an incorrect word pauses and Archie gently helps sound it out.

ARCHIE: “If you get stuck, I’ll help with the sounds, then you try again.”

A story character briefly steps out of the book and returns.

MICHAEL: “Parents and teachers can see reading progress too.”

GAME MIRROR: walkable library aisles, book doors to stories, read-along voice recognition, comprehension puzzles, badges and saved progress.

SFX: page turns, soft sparkle, fireplace/library ambience.

---

## SCENE 6 — LEARNING SHOP / SCHOOL SUPPLIES / MONEY & FRACTIONS (about 1 minute)
They leave the library through a side door into a connected shopping lane. Sweet shop, school supplies, uniforms, shoes, books, bags and celebration items are all part of the same village street.

ARCHIE: “The shops can be games too.”

Rob picks up a £2 item and a £3 item.

ROB: “How much altogether?”

ARCHIE: “Five pounds.”

A cake counter becomes a fraction activity. Archie shows halves and quarters.

MICHAEL: “Learning should feel useful, not separate from real life.”

GAME MIRROR: money, change, fractions, measurement, shopping-list reading and parent-controlled merchandise links. No child purchase without parent gate.

SFX: till chime, paper bag rustle, gentle shop chatter.

---

## SCENE 7 — MUSEUM WALK (about 1 minute)
The shopping lane leads under a stone arch directly into a museum courtyard. Archie and Rob enter a grand hall. Dinosaur, Ancient Egypt, Vikings/Sutton Hoo, Stonehenge and 1066 areas branch from the central route.

A dinosaur moves its head slightly and breathes.

ARCHIE: “Whoa!”

ROB: “Museums are full of clues. We look, ask questions and work out what they tell us.”

They inspect an artefact; a challenge card appears naturally beside it.

ROB: “Why might Vikings need shallow boats?”

Archie answers and the Viking ship display lights up.

GAME MIRROR: children physically walk between exhibits; tap artefacts for facts; answer a challenge to unlock the next gallery; teacher mode can select a history topic for a class tour.

SFX: museum reverb, dinosaur rumble kept gentle, display chime.

---

## SCENE 8 — LEARNING THEATRE (about 1 minute)
A velvet corridor from the museum opens into the theatre. Jessica and Sally, the two black dogs, sit by the stage. Curtains part.

ARCHIE: “This is where our stories and cartoons become shows.”

Rob walks onto the stage with Archie. A glowing storybook opens behind them.

ROB: “Sometimes we watch. Sometimes we join in.”

The theatre demonstrates pause, play, subtitles, question breaks and reduced-motion mode. A story pauses to ask a comprehension question.

ARCHIE answers correctly and the scene resumes.

GAME MIRROR: interactive story theatre with choices, comprehension stops, subtitles, accessible controls and saved episode progress.

SFX: curtain swish, small applause, two friendly dog barks.

---

## SCENE 9 — LEARNING FAIRGROUND + FRIENDLY SKELETON (about 1 minute)
The theatre doors open straight onto the fairground at dusk. Lights come alive. Ferris wheel turns, carousel rotates, balloons bob and stalls glow. Keep motion lively but child-friendly.

ARCHIE: “At our fair, you earn the ride by solving the challenge.”

They walk to the Ferris wheel.

ROB: “What is eight times four?”

ARCHIE: “Thirty-two!”

The wheel unlocks and begins turning.

Next is the carousel: reading challenge unlocks the horses.

At the balloon stall a spelling challenge pops the correct balloon.

Then they enter the friendly Skeleton Science House. A cheerful skeleton waves.

ARCHIE: “He’s not scary — he teaches us about our bones!”

ROB: “Which bone protects your brain?”

ARCHIE: “The skull!”

The skeleton gives a thumbs-up and the ride opens.

They pass basketball hoops, dodgems and a final knowledge coaster.

GAME MIRROR: exactly the same structure as the film. The child walks ride-to-ride; correct learning unlocks animation/rides; incorrect answers trigger hints; stars and progress save. Use friendly fairground ambience, carousel sounds and soft balloon pops.

---

## SCENE 10 — ORFORD NESS NATURE WALK (about 1 minute)
The fairground path becomes quieter and leads onto a coastal nature trail. Lights fade into sunset. Archie carries binoculars. Rob points out birds and seals. Dogs stay calm and close.

ARCHIE: “This is where we explore the real world.”

ROB: “Look carefully. What do you notice about where these animals live?”

Archie observes reeds, water and wildlife.

MICHAEL: “Science, geography and caring for nature can all be part of the same adventure.”

GAME MIRROR: binocular spotting, habitats, food chains, conservation choices, nature diary and local-history facts.

SFX: wind, reeds, distant birds, sea water.

---

## SCENE 11 — FINALE + PROGRESS + ROB TRIBUTE (about 1 minute)
The nature path loops back naturally to Orford Quay at night. Michael, Archie, Rob and the dogs stand together with warm harbour lights behind them.

MICHAEL: “Sodafom brings lessons, games, reading, homework help, teachers, parents and adventures into one place.”

ARCHIE: “And you can keep exploring every time you come back.”

Rob smiles.

ROB: “Keep asking questions.”

Archie looks at the camera.

ARCHIE: “Ready for your next adventure?”

Show progress stars, badges and certificates briefly, then fade to the Sodafom logo and tagline.

End with a respectful real-photo memorial frame:
“In loving memory of Rob Lummis.”
Supporting line: “A kind and warm man who helped so many people in Felixstowe, gave so much to charity, and always put others first.”

SFX: gentle harbour ambience, warm closing music, one soft friendly dog bark.

# GAME IMPLEMENTATION MASTER RULES
1. Film and game should share the same visual map and scene order.
2. Archie should visibly walk between activity points. Avoid dead menu-to-menu cuts when a world transition can be shown.
3. Each major attraction is both scenery and a learning gateway.
4. Correct answers trigger world movement: ride starts, ball moves, door opens, book glows, planet turns, exhibit activates.
5. Incorrect answers never punish the child; Archie gives a hint and invites another try.
6. Use touch, keyboard and microphone where practical.
7. Keep progress, stars, badges and certificates consistent across worlds.
8. Keep reduced-motion and sound controls available.
9. Keep Michael, Archie, Rob and the dogs visually consistent with the approved character references.
10. All roads lead back to the connected main world so the child feels they are exploring one place.
