# Education app benchmark — 9 October 2026

This compares published features of four established apps. It is not a hands-on evaluation of their current builds, a comparison of every education app, or evidence that Sodafom improves learning outcomes more than its competitors.

| Reference | Published strength | Sodafom response in this release | Remaining opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Khan Academy Kids](https://www.khanacademy.org/kids) | Ages 2–8, personalised paths and free exploration across books, learning and creative play. | Age-specific home missions, remembered local nicknames and saved mission progress. | Make the next incomplete course step clearer; audit the full curriculum with educators. |
| [Duolingo ABC](https://abc.duolingo.com/how-we-teach) | Short reading lessons and systematic phonics instruction. | Bounded sentence/counting/fraction mini games, optional question reading, hints and safe retries. | Expand explicit phonics progression and comprehension checkpoints. A full Sodafom course is not relabelled a five-minute lesson. |
| [Prodigy](https://www.prodigygame.com/main-en/parents/) | Learning adventures, adaptive questions and parent skill progress. | Actual game results, subject-labelled parent practice records and links to practise recorded games again. | Separate child profiles and consent-based cross-device reports; stars are not proof of curriculum mastery. |
| [ScratchJr](https://www.scratchjr.org/about/info) | Children aged 5–7 author interactive stories and games. | Existing interactive picture, word and scene activities support experimentation. | Add a genuinely child-authored scene or sequencing activity; existing rewards are not a programming environment. |

## Concrete improvements

- Learn mini games now record a correct completion in the parent practice report. Incorrect attempts create no completion; replay cannot duplicate a mission reward.
- Saved mission progress is visible for each age band.
- Recorded catalog games can be reopened from the parent report.
- The owner dashboard links all 130 catalog games, supports search/subject filters and saves private game ideas. “Available in catalog” describes registration, not a passed device or gameplay test.
- Quiz retries preserve first-attempt results and provide hints; snake and word-monster rewards follow a correct answer.

## Evidence still needed

Teacher review of every learning bank, usability sessions with children in each age band, accessibility checks with assistive technology, representative physical phone/tablet microphone tests, and measurable learning outcomes. Browser-local practice does not sync across accounts or separate siblings on a shared browser. Store submission remains a separate release with native builds, signing and final privacy disclosures.

## Age research and design decisions

Developmental guidance supports these defaults; it does not establish what every individual child prefers. Every band retains choice of activities and optional help.

| Band | Design default | Sequencing mission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5–7 | Concrete pictures, one-step counting, listening and untimed retries. | Seed, water, flower. |
| 8–9 | Grouping, patterns, clear progress and short planning tasks. | Plan, travel, explore. |
| 10–12 | Comparisons, reasoning and explaining evidence. | Question, fair test, compare evidence. |

The [Harvard executive-function activity guide](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/resources/handouts-tools/activities-guide-enhancing-and-practicing-executive-function-skills/) describes matching/memory and reasoning for 5–7, with more strategic planning for 7–12. These are developmental activity suggestions, not an app-preference survey.

[UNICEF's learning-through-play guidance](https://www.unicef.org/sites/default/files/2018-12/UNICEF-Lego-Foundation-Learning-through-Play.pdf) supports active, meaningful play and children's agency. This motivates optional activity choice and hints rather than a fixed assumption about interests.

[Nand and colleagues (2019)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40561-019-0085-2) surveyed 120 children aged 9–10 at one Auckland school. Challenge levels, feedback and graphics were prominent selected features. This limited sample supports testing those ideas; it cannot establish universal preferences or Sodafom's effectiveness.
